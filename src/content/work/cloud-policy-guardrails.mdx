During my DevSecOps internship at Cognira, **I architected and built a Policy-as-Code guardrail layer** for **Terraform-managed Azure infrastructure**. The goal was to evaluate risks **before deployment**, make findings understandable to reviewers and allow teams to choose between **non-blocking audit and strict enforcement** without losing traceability.

## 1. Architecting pre-deployment policy checks

I authored **approximately 50 custom Rego policies with deny/warn decisions**, spanning **15 governance domains** such as **access control, network exposure, sensitive-resource protection and cost governance**.

The architecture evaluates **Terraform plan JSON** using **Conftest/OPA** and external JSON/YAML policy data **before infrastructure changes are applied**. This enables review of the proposed Azure configuration without depending on the live state after deployment.

## 2. Building actionable policy reporting in Python

I developed a **Python reporting and decision engine** that parses the JSON output of Conftest, maps findings to the **affected Terraform resources**, and publishes human-readable reports in the **terminal, Markdown and GitHub Actions**.

The engine produces explicit decision states so reviewers do not have to interpret raw policy output:

- **PASSED** — the evaluation can proceed under its configured rules.
- **REVIEW REQUIRED** — findings require human review.
- **BLOCKED** — enforcing conditions prevent proceeding.
- **INCONCLUSIVE** — the evaluation cannot produce a conclusive decision.


## 3. Integrating audit and enforcement into CI/CD

I integrated the guardrail into a **GitHub Actions Terraform CI/CD pipeline**, publishing verdicts automatically and supporting two deliberate operating modes:

- **Audit mode:** surfaces findings without blocking the workflow.
- **Strict mode:** fails the pipeline and **prevents `terraform apply` while active violations remain**.

This connects infrastructure review, actionable feedback and enforcement within the delivery workflow rather than leaving policy checks as an isolated tool.

## 4. Designing exceptions without hiding violations

I implemented an **auditable, temporary exception workflow** using **ticket references, exact policy/resource/severity matching and automated expiration checks**. Approved or expired findings remain visible in reporting, preserving traceability instead of silently discarding policy results.

## What I contributed

I took the guardrail from **policy architecture and authoring** to **Python verdict generation, GitHub Actions integration and exception handling**. The outcome is a demonstrated **pre-deployment cloud governance workflow** combining policy evaluation, reviewer feedback and configurable CI controls.
